Gratuity formula

  
Gratuity = Last basic + DA × 15 ÷ 26 × Years of service

  
This formula applies when the employer is covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, which generally means it has 10 or more employees. A part year of more than six months counts as a full year. If the employer is not covered, the formula is average salary of the last 10 months × 15 ÷ 30 × completed years.

  
Example

  
Basic + DA of ₹50,000 and service of 10 years 7 months. Since 7 months is more than six, service counts as 11 years. Gratuity = 50,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 11 = ₹3,17,308. This is below ₹20 lakh, so it is fully tax-free.


  
Frequently asked questions

  
1. How is gratuity calculated?

  
For employees covered by the Gratuity Act: last drawn basic + DA × 15 ÷ 26 × years of service. A part year of more than six months counts as a full year.

  
2. What is the gratuity for ₹50,000 salary and 10 years of service?

  
₹50,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 10 = ₹2,88,462.

  
3. Who is eligible for gratuity?

  
Employees with at least 5 years of continuous service. There is no minimum in case of death or disablement, and fixed-term employees become eligible after 1 year under the new labour codes.

  
4. Is 4 years and 240 days counted as 5 years?

  
Several courts have accepted 4 years and 240 days of work in the fifth year as 5 years of continuous service, but employers' practice varies.

  
5. Why is gratuity calculated as 15/26?

  
The law gives 15 days' wages for every year of service, and a month is taken as 26 working days.

  
6. Is gratuity taxable?

  
For private employees, gratuity up to ₹20 lakh in a lifetime is tax-free and the rest is taxed as salary. Gratuity of government employees is fully tax-free.

  
7. What is the maximum gratuity?

  
The legal limit is ₹20 lakh. An employer may pay more, but the amount above ₹20 lakh is taxable.

  
8. Which salary is used for gratuity?

  
The last drawn basic salary plus dearness allowance. Under the new labour codes, if basic and DA are less than 50% of total pay, the excess allowances are added to wages, which raises gratuity.

  
9. How are months of service counted?

  
Under the Act, more than six months is rounded up to a full year, so 7 years 7 months counts as 8 years. If the employer is not covered, only completed years count.

  
10. When must the employer pay gratuity?

  
Within 30 days of it becoming payable. If paid late, the employer must also pay simple interest.
