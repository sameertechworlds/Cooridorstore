New Tax Regime Slabs FY 2026-27 with Tax Calculation Examples

Income tax slabs for FY 2026-27 under the new and old regime: zero tax up to ₹12 lakh, ₹75,000 standard deduction, marginal relief, surcharge and examples.

Updated 28 November 2026 · 8 min read · By the Cooridor Store team

Key takeaways

  • New regime slabs for FY 2026-27 are unchanged: 0% up to ₹4 lakh, then 5% steps up to 30% above ₹24 lakh.
  • Tax is nil up to ₹12 lakh taxable income because of the rebate. Salaried people pay nothing up to ₹12.75 lakh.
  • 4% cess is added on the tax. Surcharge starts above ₹50 lakh.
  • The old regime wins only when your deductions are large.

These are the same slabs as FY 2025-26. The Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from 1 April 2026, did not change the rates. The new regime is the default; you get the old regime only if you choose it.

Zero tax up to ₹12 lakh: rebate and marginal relief

A rebate of up to ₹60,000 wipes out the tax when taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000. Salaried people and pensioners also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, so a gross salary of up to ₹12,75,000 means zero tax.

Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief makes sure the tax is never more than the income above ₹12 lakh. Example: taxable income ₹12,10,000. Normal tax is ₹61,500, but relief caps it at ₹10,000, plus 4% cess = ₹10,400. Relief stops mattering at about ₹12.7 lakh taxable income.

The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as capital gains on shares and equity funds.

The old regime has a ₹50,000 standard deduction and a rebate of up to ₹12,500 (nil tax up to ₹5 lakh), but it allows HRA, home-loan interest, 80C, 80D, NPS and other deductions.

Which regime should you choose?

  • Allowed in the new regime: standard deduction, employer's NPS contribution (up to 14% of salary), family pension deduction and a few others. Most other deductions are not allowed.
  • Rule of thumb: if your total deductions (HRA, 80C, 80D, home-loan interest, NPS) are less than about ₹4 to 5 lakh, the new regime usually costs less for incomes up to ₹25 lakh.
  • Switching: salaried people can choose every year when filing the return. Those with business income can switch back to the new regime only once.

Frequently asked questions

What are the new tax regime slabs for FY 2026-27?

Nil up to ₹4 lakh, 5% for ₹4–8 lakh, 10% for ₹8–12 lakh, 15% for ₹12–16 lakh, 20% for ₹16–20 lakh, 25% for ₹20–24 lakh and 30% above ₹24 lakh.

Is ₹12 lakh income tax-free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, under the new regime the rebate makes tax nil on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. For salaried people this means a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh.

How much tax on ₹15 lakh salary?

Under the new regime, taxable income is ₹14.25 lakh after the standard deduction, and tax including cess is ₹97,500.

Is the standard deduction available in the new regime?

Yes. Salaried employees and pensioners get ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old regime.

Can I switch between old and new regime every year?

Salaried individuals without business income can choose each year. Those with business or professional income can switch back to the new regime only once.

Does the ₹12 lakh rebate apply to capital gains?

No. The rebate does not cover income taxed at special rates, such as long-term and short-term capital gains on equity.
