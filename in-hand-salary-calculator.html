What is an in-hand salary calculator?

  
An in-hand salary calculator, also called a take-home salary calculator or net salary calculator, converts your annual CTC into the amount that actually reaches your bank account every month. This CTC to in-hand salary calculator follows the rules that apply in 2026-27: the new Labour Code 50% wage rule, the ₹25,000 EPF wage ceiling from 17 September 2026, ESI, state-wise professional tax, and the income tax slabs of both the new and old regimes. It also shows your full salary breakup, the tax regime that saves more, and how much PF you will build by retirement.

What changed in salary rules for 2026-27?

  
    
   
  • Labour Codes, 50% wage rule: basic pay, DA and retaining allowance must be at least half of total pay. Higher wages mean higher PF and gratuity, so monthly take-home can fall even when CTC is unchanged.
    • 
   
  • EPF wage ceiling ₹25,000: from 17 September 2026 the ceiling moved from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month. If your PF is on the ceiling, your contribution rises from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 a month.
    • 
   
  • Gratuity after one year for fixed-term staff: the formula stays 15/26 × last wages × years of service.
    • 
   
  • Income-tax Act, 2025: it applies from 1 April 2026, with the same new-regime slabs, ₹75,000 standard deduction and tax-free income up to ₹12 lakh.

How is in-hand salary calculated from CTC?

  
In-hand salary = CTC − employer PF − gratuity − other employer costs − employee PF − ESI − professional tax − income tax. Bonus and variable pay are paid separately, so the calculator shows them after tax but keeps them out of the monthly figure.

  
Example: CTC ₹12 lakh, basic 50%, PF on the ₹25,000 ceiling, gratuity in CTC, new regime. Basic is ₹6 lakh a year (₹50,000 a month). Employer PF is ₹36,000 and gratuity ₹28,846, so gross salary is ₹11,35,154. Employee PF is ₹36,000 and professional tax depends on your state. Taxable income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction is ₹10,60,154, which is below ₹12 lakh, so income tax is nil. With ₹2,500 professional tax, monthly in-hand salary is about ₹91,388.


  
What do the terms in a salary slip mean?

  
CTC (cost to company)

  
The total amount your employer spends on you in a year. It includes your salary, employer PF, gratuity, bonus or variable pay, and sometimes insurance and PF charges. CTC is not the same as the salary you receive.

  
Basic salary and DA

  
The fixed core of your pay. Dearness allowance (DA) is mostly paid in government and some PSU jobs. Under the Labour Codes, basic + DA + retaining allowance must be at least 50% of total pay. PF and gratuity are calculated on this amount.

  
HRA (house rent allowance)

  
An allowance for rent, usually 40% of basic in non-metro cities and 50% in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. In the old regime, part of HRA is tax-free if you pay rent; in the new regime, HRA is fully taxable.

  
Special allowance

  
The balancing figure that makes your salary add up to the agreed CTC. It is fully taxable in both regimes.

  
Gross salary

  
Your total earnings before deductions: basic + HRA + allowances. It equals CTC minus employer PF, gratuity, employer ESI, employer NPS and bonus.

  
Net salary (in-hand or take-home salary)

  
The amount credited to your bank account each month after employee PF, ESI, professional tax and income tax are deducted from gross salary.

  
Employee PF (EPF)

  
12% of your PF wages deducted every month and deposited in your Employees' Provident Fund account with EPFO. It earns interest and can be withdrawn on retirement or for specified needs. You can check it with your UAN.

  
Employer PF and EPS

  
Your employer also contributes 12%. Of this, 8.33% of wages up to the wage ceiling goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), which pays a monthly pension after 58, and the rest goes to your EPF account.

  
VPF (voluntary provident fund)

  
Extra PF you choose to contribute above 12%. It earns the same interest as EPF. The employer does not match it.

  
EDLI and PF admin charges

  
EDLI is free life insurance linked to your PF account, paid for by the employer. Together with PF administration charges, it costs the employer about 1% of PF wages. Some companies include it in CTC.

  
Gratuity

  
A lump sum paid when you leave after five years of service, or after one year for fixed-term employees under the Labour Codes. Formula: 15/26 × last monthly wages × years of service. Companies usually show it in CTC at 4.81% of basic, but it is not paid monthly.

  
ESI (employees' state insurance)

  
Health insurance for employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month gross. You pay 0.75% and the employer pays 3.25%. It covers treatment at ESIC hospitals and gives cash benefits during sickness and maternity.

  
Professional tax (PT)

  
A state tax on employment, capped at ₹2,500 a year. The amount depends on your state and salary slab, and some states do not charge it.

  
Income tax (TDS on salary)

  
Your employer estimates your yearly income tax and deducts it every month as TDS. The calculator spreads tax on regular salary across 12 months and shows tax on bonus separately.

  
Standard deduction

  
A flat deduction for salaried people: ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old regime. No proof is needed.

  
Section 87A rebate

  
A tax rebate that brings tax to nil for lower incomes: up to ₹60,000 when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less in the new regime, and up to ₹12,500 when it is ₹5 lakh or less in the old regime.

  
New regime vs old regime

  
The new regime has lower slab rates but allows very few deductions. The old regime has higher rates but allows HRA, 80C (PF, PPF, LIC, ELSS), 80D health insurance, home loan interest and more. Salaried employees can choose either each year when filing their return.

  
Employer NPS

  
Your employer's contribution to the National Pension System. It is tax-free up to 14% of basic in the new regime and 10% in the old regime.

  
Bonus and variable pay

  
Performance pay that is part of CTC but paid once or a few times a year, not monthly. It is fully taxable when paid.

  
Labour Codes

  
Four laws that replaced 29 central labour laws: the Code on Wages, the Code on Social Security, the Industrial Relations Code and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code. They introduced a single definition of wages, the 50% wage rule and gratuity after one year for fixed-term staff.

How much PF is deducted from salary?

  
Employees contribute 12% of PF wages. Many employers calculate PF only on the wage ceiling, now ₹25,000 a month, which caps the employee share at ₹3,000. Others use the full basic. The employer also pays 12%, of which 8.33% (up to the ceiling) goes to the pension scheme (EPS) and the rest to your EPF account. Use the PF at retirement tab above to see how your PF can grow.

Frequently asked questions

  
1. How is in-hand salary calculated from CTC?
Start with CTC and remove the costs your employer pays on your behalf: employer PF, gratuity, employer ESI, employer NPS and PF charges. What is left is your gross salary. From gross salary, employee PF, ESI, professional tax and income tax (TDS) are deducted. The balance, divided by 12, is your monthly in-hand salary. Bonus and variable pay are paid separately.

  
2. What is the difference between CTC, gross salary and net salary?
CTC (cost to company) is everything the employer spends on you in a year, including employer PF, gratuity and bonus. Gross salary is CTC minus those employer-side costs, and it is what appears as total earnings on your payslip. Net salary, also called in-hand or take-home salary, is gross salary minus employee PF, ESI, professional tax and income tax.

  
3. What is the 50% wage rule under the new Labour Codes?
Under the Labour Codes, wages (basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance) must be at least 50% of total remuneration. If allowances such as HRA and special allowance are more than half, the extra is treated as wages. Because PF and gratuity are calculated on wages, they rise, and monthly take-home can fall even when CTC stays the same.

  
4. What is the EPF wage ceiling from September 2026?
The EPF wage ceiling was raised from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month with effect from 17 September 2026. If your employer calculates PF on the ceiling, the maximum employee contribution is now ₹3,000 a month (12% of ₹25,000), up from ₹1,800.

  
5. How much PF is deducted from my salary?
The employee contributes 12% of PF wages (basic plus DA). If PF is on the wage ceiling, the deduction is at most ₹3,000 a month; if it is on full basic, it is 12% of your whole basic. The employer adds another 12%, of which 8.33% of wages up to the ceiling goes to the pension scheme (EPS) and the rest to your EPF account.

  
6. Which tax regime gives higher in-hand salary in 2026-27?
For most salaried people without large deductions, the new regime gives higher take-home pay, because it has lower rates, a ₹75,000 standard deduction and no tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. The old regime can work out better if you claim large HRA, 80C, 80D and home loan deductions. This calculator works out both and picks the cheaper one.

  
7. Is salary up to ₹12 lakh really tax-free?
Under the new regime, the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 makes tax nil when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. With the ₹75,000 standard deduction, a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh can be tax-free. Just above the limit, marginal relief ensures tax does not exceed the income above ₹12 lakh. Employee PF does not reduce taxable income in the new regime.

  
8. Is ESI deducted from my salary?
ESI applies when gross monthly wages are ₹21,000 or less. The employee pays 0.75% and the employer pays 3.25% of gross wages. ESI gives medical care and cash benefits through ESIC hospitals and dispensaries.

  
9. How much professional tax is deducted?
Professional tax is a state tax capped at ₹2,500 a year. Maharashtra, Karnataka and several other states charge up to ₹2,500, states such as West Bengal, Telangana and Gujarat up to ₹2,400, and Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttarakhand do not levy it. In the old regime, professional tax is deductible from taxable income.

  
10. Why is my in-hand salary less than CTC divided by 12?
CTC includes items that never reach your monthly pay: employer PF, gratuity (paid only when you leave), bonus or variable pay, and sometimes insurance or PF charges. Your own PF, professional tax and income tax are then deducted. That is why monthly in-hand is usually 70% to 90% of CTC divided by 12.
