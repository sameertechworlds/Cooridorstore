What is a SIP calculator?

A SIP calculator is a free online tool that shows how much your systematic investment plan (SIP) in a mutual fund can grow to. This SIP return calculator goes further than most: it works as a step-up SIP calculator, lumpsum calculator, goal-based SIP planner and SWP calculator in one place, adjusts the result for inflation, deducts LTCG tax under 2026-27 rules, and lets you backtest a SIP in any real mutual fund using its actual NAV history.

  1. SIP: set your monthly investment, expected return, time period and yearly step-up. Add a one-time amount if you are starting with savings.
  2. Lumpsum: enter a one-time investment to see its future value and compare it with spreading the same money as a SIP.
  3. Goal planner: enter a target such as ₹1 crore for retirement or ₹25 lakh for your child's education, and see the monthly SIP or lumpsum needed.

What is the SIP formula?

Maturity value = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r), where P is the monthly investment, r is the expected annual return ÷ 12 and n is the number of months. Each instalment is assumed to be invested at the start of the month.

Example: ₹10,000 a month for 15 years at 12% means 180 instalments and a total investment of ₹18,00,000. The estimated maturity value is about ₹50,45,760, so the estimated gain is about ₹32,45,760.

What is a step-up SIP and why does it matter?

A step-up SIP (top-up SIP) raises your monthly instalment every year, usually by 5% to 15%, in step with salary increases. In the example above, a flat SIP of ₹10,000 grows to about ₹50.46 lakh, while the same SIP with a 10% yearly step-up grows to about ₹86.84 lakh on a total investment of about ₹38.13 lakh. The Power of step-up card shows the extra amount for your own numbers.

SIP vs lumpsum: which is better?

A lumpsum works best when you already have the money and markets rise steadily afterwards. A SIP suits regular income, builds discipline and averages your purchase cost through market ups and downs (rupee cost averaging). Many investors keep a lumpsum in a liquid fund and move it into equity through a systematic transfer plan (STP) over 6 to 12 months.

What do these mutual fund terms mean?

SIP (systematic investment plan)

A fixed amount invested in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly, through an auto-debit from your bank account.

NAV (net asset value)

The price of one unit of a mutual fund, declared every business day. Your SIP buys units at that day's NAV.

Units

Your share of the fund. Units = amount invested ÷ NAV. Value = units × current NAV.

CAGR

Compound annual growth rate: the yearly growth rate of a single investment between two dates. The fund's 1, 3, 5 and 10-year CAGR are shown in the backtest.

XIRR

The annual return on cash flows made on different dates, such as SIP instalments. It is the right measure of SIP performance.

Step-up (top-up) SIP

A SIP whose amount rises by a fixed percentage every year.

Lumpsum

A one-time investment of the whole amount.

SWP and STP

A systematic withdrawal plan takes a fixed amount out every month. A systematic transfer plan moves money from one fund to another in instalments.

Direct vs regular plan

A direct plan is bought straight from the fund house with no distributor commission, so its expense ratio is lower. A regular plan is bought through a distributor and costs more every year.

Expense ratio

The annual fee charged by the fund as a percentage of your investment. It is already reflected in the NAV.

Exit load

A fee some funds charge if you redeem within a set period, often 1% within one year.

Index fund

A fund that copies an index such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex, usually with a low expense ratio.

ELSS

An equity-linked savings scheme: an equity fund with a three-year lock-in that qualifies for the Section 80C deduction in the old tax regime.

Inflation

The yearly rise in prices that reduces what your money can buy. At 6% inflation, ₹1 crore 20 years from now buys roughly what ₹31 lakh buys today.

LTCG and STCG

Long-term and short-term capital gains: the profit when you sell units, taxed at different rates depending on how long you held them.

KYC

Know your customer: a one-time identity check (PAN, Aadhaar and address) needed before you invest in any mutual fund.

Frequently asked questions

1. What is a SIP calculator and how does it work?

A SIP calculator estimates how much your monthly investment in a mutual fund can grow to. You enter the monthly amount, expected annual return and time period, and it applies monthly compounding to show the total invested, estimated returns and maturity value. This calculator also handles yearly step-up, a one-time amount, inflation and LTCG tax.

2. What is the formula for SIP returns?

Maturity value = P × [((1 + r)^n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r), where P is the monthly SIP, r is the expected annual return divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly instalments. For example, ₹10,000 a month for 15 years at 12% grows to about ₹50.46 lakh on an investment of ₹18 lakh.

3. What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP, also called a top-up SIP, increases your monthly instalment by a fixed percentage every year, usually in line with salary hikes. ₹10,000 a month for 15 years at 12% grows to about ₹50.46 lakh as a flat SIP, but to about ₹86.84 lakh with a 10% yearly step-up.

4. How much SIP is needed for ₹1 crore?

At an expected 12% annual return, you need about ₹43,041 a month for 10 years, ₹19,819 for 15 years, ₹10,009 for 20 years, ₹5,270 for 25 years or ₹2,833 for 30 years. Use the Goal planner tab to include inflation, step-up and existing savings.

5. SIP or lumpsum: which is better?

A lumpsum earns more when markets rise steadily after you invest, because all the money is invested from day one. A SIP spreads purchases over time, buying more units when prices are low (rupee cost averaging), which reduces the risk of investing everything at a market peak. Most salaried investors use SIPs; a lumpsum suits idle cash, often moved in gradually through a systematic transfer plan.

6. How are SIP returns taxed in 2026-27?

For equity mutual funds, units held for more than 12 months give long-term capital gains taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year; units held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period. Gains on debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your income tax slab rate. Cess applies on top.

7. What is XIRR in SIP?

XIRR (extended internal rate of return) is the annualised return on investments made on different dates, such as monthly SIP instalments. It is the correct way to measure SIP performance, because simple CAGR assumes all the money was invested on the first day. The fund backtest on this page shows the XIRR of a real SIP.

8. What is SWP and how is it different from SIP?

A SIP puts a fixed amount into a fund every month; an SWP (systematic withdrawal plan) takes a fixed amount out every month while the rest stays invested. SWP is used for regular income after retirement. The SWP tab shows how long your corpus will last and the maximum safe monthly withdrawal.

9. Is the mutual fund data on this page live?

Yes. The fund search and backtest fetch the latest NAV and full NAV history from AMFI data through the free mfapi.in service, which updates several times a day. The calculator tabs use the return you enter, not live data.

10. Can I lose money in a SIP?

Yes. SIPs invest in market-linked mutual funds, so the value can fall below the amount invested, especially over short periods or in equity funds during a market fall. Over longer periods diversified equity funds have historically smoothed out volatility, but past returns do not guarantee future returns. Read the scheme documents and check your risk profile before investing.
