GST Rates 2026: New Slab List (5%, 18%, 40%) with Item-Wise Rates

Latest GST rates in India for 2026 after GST 2.0: nil, 5%, 18% and 40% slabs with item lists, old vs new rates, 3% on gold and how to calculate GST.

Updated 28 November 2026 · 7 min read · By the Cooridor Store team

Key takeaways

  • Since 22 September 2025, GST has two main rates, 5% and 18%, and a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods.
  • The old 12% and 28% slabs were removed. Most items moved down to 5% or 18%.
  • Gold and silver stay at 3%. Many essentials, notebooks and individual insurance are nil.
  • Add GST: price × (1 + rate). Remove GST: total ÷ (1 + rate).

What changed on 22 September 2025

The 56th GST Council meeting approved "GST 2.0", which simplified the rate structure from four main slabs (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%) to two main slabs, 5% and 18%. A special 40% rate now applies to a short list of luxury and sin goods. The new rates took effect on 22 September 2025 and continue through 2026.

The aim was fewer classification disputes and lower tax on everyday goods. For businesses, it also means FY 2025-26 has two rate periods, so invoices before and after 22 September 2025 can carry different rates for the same item.

Services with a choice of rate

Some services can choose between two rates. Passenger transport by road and container train operators can charge 5% without input tax credit or 18% with full credit. Pick the option that gives the lower total cost after credit. Once chosen, use it consistently for the year.

Frequently asked questions

How many GST slabs are there in 2026?

There are two main slabs, 5% and 18%, a special 40% rate for luxury and sin goods, a nil rate for essentials and special rates such as 3% for gold and silver.

When did the new GST rates start?

The GST 2.0 rates took effect on 22 September 2025.

Is the 12% GST slab removed?

Yes. Items in the old 12% slab mostly moved to 5%, and a few moved to 18%.

What is the GST rate on gold?

Gold and silver, including jewellery, attract 3% GST. Making charges billed separately are taxed at the rate for job work.

Is health insurance taxable under GST now?

Individual health and life insurance policies are exempt from GST from 22 September 2025.

How do I remove GST from a price?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate. For 18%, divide by 1.18. ₹11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000, so GST is ₹1,800.
