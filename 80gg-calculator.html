What is Section 80GG?

  
Section 80GG gives a deduction for rent paid on the house you live in, if you do not get House Rent Allowance (HRA). It helps self-employed people, professionals, and salaried people whose salary has no HRA. Under the Income-tax Act 2025, which applies from 1 April 2026, the same deduction is in Section 134, with the same limits.

Who is eligible for Section 80GG?

  
    
   
  • You are an individual (salaried or self-employed) who pays rent for your own residence.
    • 
   
  • You do not receive HRA from an employer.
    • 
   
  • You, your spouse, your minor child or your HUF do not own a house in the place where you live or work.
    • 
   
  • If you own a house somewhere else, you must not be claiming it as self-occupied.

  • You choose the old tax regime.

Frequently asked questions

  
1. What is the maximum deduction under Section 80GG?
The least of: rent paid minus 10% of adjusted total income, ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 a year), or 25% of adjusted total income. For most people the ₹60,000 cap is the limit that applies.

  
2. Who is eligible for Section 80GG?
Individuals who pay rent for their home and do not receive HRA, including self-employed people and salaried people without HRA, who choose the old regime and do not own a house where they live or work.

  
3. Can I claim 80GG in the new tax regime?
No. Like most deductions, 80GG (Section 134) is available only under the old tax regime. Use our income tax calculator to see which regime is better for you.

  
4. What is Form 10BA and how do I file it?
Form 10BA is the declaration of rent paid. File it on the income tax e-filing portal before you file your return, giving details such as the rented address, rent paid and the landlord's name and address.

  
5. 80GG vs HRA: can I claim both?
No. 80GG is only for people who do not receive HRA. If your salary includes HRA, claim the HRA exemption instead. Try our HRA calculator.

  
6. Can I claim 80GG for rent paid to my parents?
Yes, if the rent is genuinely paid, preferably by bank transfer, backed by a rent agreement and receipts, and your parents show the rent as income in their return. You cannot pay rent to your spouse for a house you share as a family.

  
7. Can self-employed people and freelancers claim 80GG?
Yes. Self-employed people, freelancers and professionals who pay rent for their home can claim it under the old regime if the other conditions are met.

  
8. What documents are required for 80GG?
Form 10BA filed on the portal, a rent agreement, monthly rent receipts and proof of payment such as bank or UPI statements. Our rent receipt generator can make the receipts.

  
9. What is the new section number of 80GG?
From 1 April 2026, Section 80GG of the Income-tax Act 1961 is Section 134 of the Income-tax Act 2025. The ₹5,000 per month cap and the 10% and 25% tests are unchanged.

  
10. Can I claim 80GG if I own a house in another city?
Yes, as long as the house is not in the place where you live or work and you are not claiming it as self-occupied in your return.
