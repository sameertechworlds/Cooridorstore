What is GST?

    
Goods and Services Tax (GST) is an indirect tax charged on the supply of goods and services in India. It replaced older taxes like VAT, excise and service tax. The seller collects GST from the buyer and deposits it with the government.


    
GST formulas

    
1. Adding GST (price is exclusive of GST)

    
GST amount = Original price × GST rate ÷ 100
Total price = Original price + GST amount

    
2. Removing GST (price is inclusive of GST)

    
Base price = Total price × 100 ÷ (100 + GST rate)
GST amount = Total price − Base price


    
Worked examples

    
Example 1: Add 18% GST to ₹10,000

    
GST = 10,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹1,800. Total = 10,000 + 1,800 = ₹11,800. If the sale is within the same state, CGST = ₹900 and SGST = ₹900.

    
Example 2: Remove 18% GST from ₹11,800

    
Base price = 11,800 × 100 ÷ 118 = ₹10,000. GST = 11,800 − 10,000 = ₹1,800.

    
Example 3: Inter-state sale of ₹50,000 at 5%

    
GST = 50,000 × 5 ÷ 100 = ₹2,500, charged fully as IGST. Total = ₹52,500.

Current GST rates in India (GST 2.0)

    
From 22 September 2025, GST moved to two main rates, 5% and 18%, with a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods. The 12% and 28% slabs were removed for almost all items. From 1 February 2026, tobacco products and pan masala also moved from 28% plus compensation cess to 40%.

    
RateUsed for

    
0% (exempt)Many essentials such as fresh food items and some basic services

    
5%Merit rate: most everyday goods and many services

    
18%Standard rate: most goods and services

    
40%De-merit rate: luxury and sin goods such as tobacco, pan masala and certain cars

    
3% and 0.25%Special rates: gold, silver and jewellery (3%); rough diamonds and precious stones (0.25%)

    
Invoices from before these changes may still show 12% or 28%. Use the "Other rate" box above for such cases, and always confirm the rate for your HSN or SAC code on the official CBIC GST site.


    
Frequently asked questions

    
How do I calculate GST on a GST-inclusive price?

    
Divide the total by (100 + rate) and multiply by 100 to get the base price. The difference is the GST.

    
Is this GST calculator free?

    
Yes, it is completely free, needs no login, and runs entirely in your browser.

    
Are the 12% and 28% GST slabs still there?

    
For almost all goods and services, no. They were merged into 5%, 18% and 40% under GST 2.0 from 22 September 2025, and tobacco moved to 40% from 1 February 2026. For an old invoice, type 12 or 28 in the Other rate box.
