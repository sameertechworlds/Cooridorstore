What does this income tax calculator do?

  
This free income tax calculator works out your tax for FY 2026-27 (Tax Year 2026-27) and FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) under both the new and the old regime. Unlike a simple slab calculator, it handles the parts where most online calculators go wrong: the ₹12 lakh rebate with marginal relief, HRA for the eight metro cities, house property loss, capital gains at special rates, surcharge with marginal relief, the basic exemption set-off against capital gains, and rounding under sections 288A and 288B. It then compares both regimes, shows each step, and tells you the tax still payable or the refund after TDS.

  
Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded or stored.


  
What changed for FY 2026-27?

  
    
   
  • New law, same rates. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. The CBDT announced that the "Income-tax Act, 2025 comes into force from 1st April, 2026" (CBDT press release). Slab rates did not change.
    • 
   
  • "Tax Year" replaces "previous year" and "assessment year". Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 is Tax Year 2026-27, with the return filed in 2027.
    • 
   
  • HRA for 8 cities. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad now get the 50% HRA limit along with Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai.
    • 
   
  • New section numbers. Deductions and TDS have new section numbers under the 2025 Act. This page uses the familiar old numbers (80C, 80D, 87A) because that is what most people search for.
    •

  1. Add up income under five heads: salary, house property, business or profession, capital gains, and other sources.
    2. 
   
  2. Reduce salary by the standard deduction (₹75,000 new, ₹50,000 old), exempt HRA (old regime), employer NPS and professional tax (old regime).
    3. 
   
  3. Set off house property loss up to ₹2 lakh against other income (old regime only).

  4. Subtract deductions such as 80C, 80D and 80CCD(1B) in the old regime to reach taxable income. Round it to the nearest ₹10.

  5. Apply slab rates to normal income and special rates to capital gains and crypto.

  6. Subtract the 87A rebate (residents only) and apply marginal relief where income is just above ₹12 lakh.

  7. Add surcharge if total income is above ₹50 lakh, with marginal relief at each threshold.
    8. 
   
  8. Add 4% health and education cess, round to the nearest ₹10, and subtract TDS and advance tax already paid.

How do the ₹12 lakh rebate and marginal relief work?

  
Under the new regime, a resident whose total income is up to ₹12 lakh gets a rebate of up to ₹60,000, so the tax becomes nil. With the ₹75,000 standard deduction, salary up to ₹12.75 lakh is tax-free. If income goes slightly above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh. For example, on taxable income of ₹12,10,000 the slab tax is ₹61,500, but you pay only ₹10,000 plus cess. The old regime rebate is up to ₹12,500 for income up to ₹5 lakh.

  
The rebate does not reduce tax on long-term capital gains on equity, crypto income, or (in the new regime) other special-rate income. That is why someone with ₹10 lakh salary and large share gains can still owe tax.

What do the terms mean?

  
Gross total income
Total of all five income heads after exemptions such as HRA and the standard deduction, before Chapter VI-A deductions like 80C.

  
Taxable income
Gross total income minus deductions, rounded to the nearest ₹10. Slab rates apply to this.

  
Rebate under section 87A
A reduction in tax for residents with income up to ₹12 lakh (new regime) or ₹5 lakh (old regime).

  
Marginal relief
A cap that stops tax from jumping by more than the extra income when you cross the rebate or surcharge limits.

  
Surcharge
An extra tax on tax for incomes above ₹50 lakh.

  
Cess
Health and education cess of 4% on tax plus surcharge.

  
TDS
Tax deducted at source by your employer, bank or client, which counts towards your total tax.

  
Advance tax
Tax paid in instalments during the year when the tax due after TDS is ₹10,000 or more.


  
Frequently asked questions

  
How do I calculate income tax for FY 2026-27?
Add your income from all sources, subtract the standard deduction and other allowed deductions, apply the slab rates of your chosen regime, subtract the 87A rebate if eligible, add surcharge if income is above ₹50 lakh, and add 4% cess. The calculator above does every step and shows the working.

  
Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free?
Yes, in the new regime for residents. The rebate makes tax nil on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh, and salaried people with the ₹75,000 standard deduction pay nothing up to ₹12.75 lakh, as long as there is no special-rate income such as capital gains.

  
What is marginal relief in the new regime?
If income is just over ₹12 lakh, tax is limited to the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh. On ₹12.1 lakh, for example, tax is ₹10,000 plus cess instead of ₹61,500.

  
How much tax will I pay on a ₹15 lakh salary?
Under the new regime, ₹97,500 including cess. Taxable income is ₹14.25 lakh after the ₹75,000 standard deduction.

  
How much tax on a ₹20 lakh salary?
₹1,92,400 under the new regime, including cess, with no other deductions.

  
Which regime is better for me?
Enter your numbers above. The calculator shows both and also tells you how many more old-regime deductions you would need for the old regime to win. For most people with deductions under ₹4–5 lakh, the new regime is cheaper.

  
Can I claim HRA and home loan interest in the new regime?
No. HRA exemption and interest on a self-occupied home loan are allowed only in the old regime. Interest on a let-out house is allowed in the new regime but a loss cannot be set off.

  
Does the rebate apply to capital gains?
No. The rebate does not cover tax on equity LTCG, crypto, or special-rate income in the new regime. Unused basic exemption can still be set off against capital gains for residents.

  
Can I switch between the old and new regime every year?
Salaried people and pensioners without business income can choose every year while filing. Those with business income can leave the new regime and come back only once.

  
Why is my result different from my employer's TDS?
Employers estimate tax on salary only and may not know about your interest, rent, capital gains or deductions you did not declare. Your final tax is based on total income, which is what this calculator works out.
